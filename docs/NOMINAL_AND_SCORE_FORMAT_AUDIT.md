## Nominal terminology findings

Human turn labels mean `Sean's turn`, `Troy's turn`, `Matt's turn`, `Jess's turn`, `Classics week` for historical positions 1–5. These identify historical position, not necessarily actual host. Current identity must continue to honour `human_order` swaps through `effectiveMember`; do not relabel a swapped current turn using fixed positional names. Film lineup positions and cycle ordinals are useful different concepts and are outside this cleanup.

## Score formatting findings

Pipeline evidence: **I** = MDBList IMDb scale 10 and OMDb `imdbRating` scale 10 pass through `record()`; valid decimal numbers/strings are preserved. **A** = MDBList `popcorn`, `tomatoesaudience`, `audience` map to RT audience scale 100; no OMDb audience fallback. **C** = MDBList `tomatoes` maps to RT critic scale 100; OMDb RT percentage regex explicitly accepts decimals. See `worker/src/providers/{mdblist,omdb,ratings}.ts`. **L** = `scripts/import/workbook.ts:33–36,109–112` accepts decimal numeric cells, strings and cached formula results for IMDb/RT B–D on scale 100; `model.ts:7` has no integer requirement, `resolution.ts:93` and `apply.ts:52` preserve them. These prove accepted fractional observations, not that any private archive/provider response actually contains fractional RT percentages.

Snapshots use REAL raw/normalised columns (`worker/migrations/0004_import_provenance.sql:3–6`); `Repository.appendScores` binds numbers unchanged (`worker/src/repository.ts:118–122`). `latestScores` chooses usable observations by retrieval precedence and capture time, then legacy preference/ordinal. `scoreValue` recomputes raw/scale ×100 where scale exists, otherwise accepts stored normalised value (`shared/ranking.ts:4–28`); it never rounds. Decimal-preserving display should suppress floating-point noise, not round every accepted fractional observation to an integer.

## Summary decisions

- Total user-visible nominal occurrences: **22 source presentation sites**, including conditional/defensive errors and one generated JSON evidence surface; not a count of repeated runtime cards/options.
- REMOVE ENTIRELY: **2**.
- REPLACE WITH HUMAN TURN LABEL: **8**.
- KEEP INTERNAL ONLY: **12**; preserve mechanics, humanise errors/evidence.
- All score display locations found: **10 source rendering sites** — ranking source summary, source breakdown, raw Film Detail ratings, normalised Film Detail ratings, Metrics Top/Bottom individual IMDb, overall IMDb mean, genre IMDb mean, final ranking score, sum of squares, unseen multiplier. Shared ranking sites cover Home shortlist, all Classics tabs and Film Detail; no scores render in Event, Builder, History, Seen It? or external previews. Genre share percentages are not source ratings.
- Safely integer-only metrics across current ingestion/storage: **none**. RT audience/critic scale 100 does not impose integrality; their provider adapters and historical importer admit and preserve decimal observations. Whole values can safely omit `.0` individually, without asserting a metric-wide invariant.
- Legitimately fractional: native IMDb /10, legacy IMDb and any decimal-preserving normalised source input; derived averages, squared totals and ranking scores/multipliers. RT fractions are accepted end-to-end, but their actual upstream/archive occurrence and semantic validity are unverified; no blanket integer conversion is justified by this source audit.
- Decisions resolved for this implementation: accepted fractions remain valid; `/100` display retains at most one decimal; known audit evidence is human-readable with stored JSON untouched. Original decision questions: whether fractional RT observations should be recognised as meaningful or treated as invalid source data (requires explicit evidence/contract decision, not display rounding); maximum displayed precision for accepted provider/legacy fractions; how to expose human-readable History change evidence while retaining original JSON internally. Fixed historical turn labels must remain distinct from swapped effective current identity and actual historical host.
